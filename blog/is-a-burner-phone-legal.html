Is a Burner Phone Legal?

Yes. In the United States, there is nothing inherently illegal about acquiring or using a phone that is not tied to your identity. Prepaid phones are sold openly in stores across the country. What the law cares about is what you do with a phone — not whether the phone is tied to your name.

The short version

What the law actually looks at

The law does not ban anonymous phones. It bans criminal acts committed with any phone. Keeping your communications private, researching freely, and speaking without your name attached are lawful activities. Committing fraud, making threats, harassing someone, or coordinating a crime is illegal no matter what phone you use — a burner phone changes nothing about that.

The misconception

Why people think burner phones are illegal

Mostly, television. In crime dramas, a "burner" is the phone a criminal uses and throws away, so the word picked up a criminal association. The object itself is just a prepaid phone — the same kind sold by the millions every year for travel, for kids, as backup phones, and for budgeting. Nothing about buying one breaks the law.

No registration

Do you have to register a burner phone?

In the United States, no federal law requires you to show ID or register when you buy a prepaid phone. Some other countries do require SIM registration, but the U.S. does not. Postpaid plans are different — a carrier signing you to a monthly contract will ask for your details — but a prepaid phone bought outright carries no such requirement.

What a burner phone does not do

A burner phone does not make illegal activity legal, and it does not make you invisible to a criminal investigation. If someone commits a crime, law enforcement has many tools beyond the phone itself.
