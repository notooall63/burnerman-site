What Happens to a Burner Phone When You're Done With It?

You send it back. Every Burner Man phone ships with a prepaid return path. Email your client number with a request to recycle, receive a prepaid label, and send the phone in. Working phones are wiped and returned to the anonymizing pool. Broken ones are recycled in accordance with applicable laws. Nothing is resold, nothing is repurposed, and no loose ends are left behind.

The process

Returning a phone, step by step

  • Step 1 — Stop using it. Power the phone down and return it to its Faraday pouch. Do not keep using it over Wi-Fi indefinitely after the cellular service expires — that stretches one device past its lifespan and builds exactly the pattern the rotation system exists to prevent.
  • Step 2 — Email your client number. Send your client number with a request to recycle, and you will receive a prepaid label for a small return package.
  • Step 3 — Send it back. Ship the phone on the prepaid path. If you are on a renewal interval, a freshly randomized device arrives on your schedule of 1, 2, or 4 weeks, so there is no gap in your private communications.
  • Step 4 — The phone is processed. What happens next depends on the phone's condition, covered below.

The discount is the only record kept from a return: the email address and its corresponding discount, held only until it is redeemed. Everything else about the transaction is purged once it is no longer operationally necessary.

Why it matters

Why not just keep it, or throw it away?

A phone sitting in a drawer is a loose end. It still carries its identifiers and its history, and it is one drawer-cleaning, one burglary, or one curious hand away from someone else's possession. Selling or trading it in is worse: it creates a new record with your name on it and passes the device — and whatever patterns it carries — to a stranger. Throwing it in the trash is worse still: batteries and electronics do not belong in landfill, and the device is not destroyed, just discarded.

Returning it closes the loop in both directions. It reduces environmental impact, and it ensures the device is never resold or repurposed in ways that could reintroduce attribution risk. The faster you use a particular phone and recycle it, the more private your communications remain — returning devices for recycling reduces your risk surface and closes the loop.

Common questions

Questions people ask

What if the phone is broken?

Send it back anyway. Non-functioning phones can still be recycled on your behalf in accordance with applicable laws. If the device is intact, even if it does not work, it may still qualify you for a smaller discount on a future purchase.

What if the phone wasn't bought from Burner Man?

It can still be recycled on your behalf under the same process. The return path is not limited to Burner Man devices — proper disposal matters regardless of where a phone came from.

What if I lose the phone?

Operationally, that is preferable to losing a phone tied to your identity. There is no account or profile to reset — the device simply ceases to be part of your workflow. The design assumes devices may be lost or destroyed without compromising your identity.
